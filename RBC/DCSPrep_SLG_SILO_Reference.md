### CSE1IIT — Inside Information Technology
**Course:** RBC (Core, Year 1 Sem 1)  
**AQF Level:** 5 | **Credit Points:** 15 | **Delivery:** Blended (Day)  
**Prerequisites:** None

**About this subject:**  
Students are introduced to the rapidly expanding field of information technology including hardware and software, computer networks, information systems, databases, the Internet, World Wide Web and HTML. Relevant to students across health sciences, business, engineering and technology. Students acquire knowledge and practical IT skills and can apply them to their field of study.

**SILOs — upon successful completion, students should be able to:**
1. Analyse how a computer together with its input/output devices processes and delivers data/information to users.
2. Explain the causes for the present information/digital age by advances in technologies in computer, information systems, computer networks, Internet, and the World Wide Web.
3. Apply the knowledge in Internet, World Wide Web and HTML to designing and constructing a Web system.
4. Apply the knowledge in information technology to addressing/solving real-life problems.

**Weekly topics:**
- Week 1: Lectures, Tutorial
- Weeks 2–12: Lectures, Lab (12 weeks total, 48 contact hours)

**Topics covered:** Computer hardware (CPU, I/O, storage), computer networks (LAN/WAN), information systems, databases, Internet, World Wide Web, HTML

**DCSPrep mapping:** Hardware fundamentals (CPU, devices, storage), networking concepts (LAN/WAN), web basics — maps to A+/L1 content on endpoint and networking. SILO 1 and 4 are strong anchors for troubleshooting module framing.

### CSE1ICB — Introduction to Cybersecurity
**Course:** RBC (Core, Year 1 Sem 1)  
**AQF Level:** UG | **Credit Points:** 15 | **Delivery:** Face-to-face / Online  
**Prerequisites:** None

**About this subject:**  
In the modern Internet era, industries and organisations need to be prepared to defend against cyber threats and attacks. Covers key principles and best practices of cybersecurity including information security, ethical and legal practices, mitigating cyber vulnerabilities, and incident response and analysis. Introduces the broad discipline of cybersecurity and how to ensure privacy, reliability, confidentiality, and integrity of information systems.

**SILOs — upon successful completion, students should be able to:**
1. Describe key emerging cybersecurity practices, regulations and standards.
2. Demonstrate foundation skills in safeguarding data, systems and networks.
3. Compare approaches for cyber risk management used to address real-world problems.
4. Identify approaches to digital forensics, application security and network security in the context of cyberspace.
5. Show understanding of data security, web security and cryptography and possible solutions to cyber threats.

**Weekly topics:**
- Week 1: The Cyber Story
- Week 2: Application Security
- Week 3–4: Network Security
- (continuing to Week 12)

**DCSPrep mapping:** Direct fit for a cybersecurity awareness module. SILOs 1–3 are core knowledge; SILOs 4–5 are stretch. Relevant to school IT context: device security, account hygiene, phishing, data handling. Cryptography and forensics are stretch/L2 territory.

### CSE1PE — Programming Environment
**Course:** RBC (Core, Year 1 Sem 1)  
**AQF Level:** 5 | **Credit Points:** 15 | **Delivery:** Blended  
**Prerequisites:** None

**About this subject:**  
Introduces the steps involved in designing and creating working software solutions to simple programming problems. Uses flowcharts for methodical software design and introduces the Python programming language. Assumes no previous exposure to programming.

**SILOs — upon successful completion, students should be able to:**
1. Analyse a data processing problem to correctly identify both the data and high-level processing involved.
2. Apply the 3 basic programming constructs of sequence, selection, and iteration to design computational solutions.
3. Use basic data structures such as lists and dictionaries to solve batch data processing problems.
4. Implement executable code in the Python programming language to solve computational problems.

**Weekly topics:**
1. Algorithms and Flowcharts
2. Statements and Expressions
3. Booleans and Conditional Execution
4. Iteration _(mid-semester break follows)_
5. Functions and Objects
6. Strings and Files
7. Data Structures
8. Software Errors _(Programming assignment released)_
9. Using Modules
10. Structuring and Documenting Code
11. Algorithm Design Strategies
12. Revision

**DCSPrep mapping:** Scripting/automation context. For DCS, the practical angle is: understanding what scripts do (batch jobs, automation, PowerShell logic patterns), even if the student isn't writing Python. SILO 2 (sequence/selection/iteration) helps demystify what IT scripts are doing. Could support a "read and understand a script" module.

### STA1DCT — Data-Based Critical Thinking
**Course:** RBC (Core, Year 1 Sem 1)  
**AQF Level:** UG | **Credit Points:** 15 | **Delivery:** Day  
**Prerequisites:** None

**About this subject:**  
Helps students evaluate data-based evidence encountered in everyday life. Provides fundamental numeracy skills for professionals who need to evaluate data-based arguments. Four themes: gathering useful data, turning data into information, probability, and from data to decision making.

**SILOs — upon successful completion, students should be able to:**
1. Critique data-based conclusions that are reported in the media and similar outlets.
2. Interpret/derive simple numeric and graphical statistical summary measures of data.
3. Calculate probabilities in a variety of scenarios that may be used for informed decision making.
4. Identify and discuss common misconceptions of probability that can lead to poor decisions.

**Weekly topics (assessed):**
- 60% 2-hour examination (SILOs 1, 2, 3, 4)
- 30% Assignments (5 assignments, odd weeks from Week 3)
- 10% Online numeracy skills quizzes (SILO 2)

**DCSPrep mapping:** Less directly applicable to IT support practice, but the critical thinking framing (SILO 1) is relevant: reading metrics, interpreting error rates, understanding uptime percentages, evaluating vendor claims. Could underpin a "reading dashboards and logs" module.

### CSE1OOF — Object-Oriented Programming Fundamentals
**Course:** RBC (Core, Year 1)  
**AQF Level:** 5 | **Credit Points:** 15 | **Delivery:** Blended  
**Prerequisites:** CSE1PE (or admitted into Bachelor of Computer Science)

**About this subject:**  
Introduces computers, object-oriented concepts and programming using Java. Covers classes and objects, primitive data types, flow of control, methods, basic input/output and arrays. Unix operating system introduced. Software engineering principles including coding standards, class design and testing.

**SILOs — upon successful completion, students should be able to:**
1. Use an operating system and development environment to code, debug and execute Java programs that solve a given problem.
2. Analyse a problem and construct a logical solution suitable for implementing as a computer program.
3. Develop a basic understanding of the object-oriented concepts of objects and classes.
4. Find and use existing Java components provided in code libraries.
5. Write Java code that uses control structures, classes and arrays.
6. Design and execute test plans.

**Weekly topics:**
- Week 0: Pre-reading (examinable)
- Weeks 1–12: Lectures, Lecture/Workshops, Labs (5 hours/week)

**DCSPrep mapping:** SILO 1 (OS environment, debugging) and SILO 6 (test plans) are transferable IT skills. For DCS context, the "read code, understand what it does, know how to test something works" framing is more relevant than writing Java. Useful background for understanding what developers mean in tickets.

### CSE1IS — Information Systems
**Course:** RBC  
**AQF Level:** 5 | **Credit Points:** 15 | **Delivery:** Day  
**Prerequisites:** CSE1ITF or CSE1IIT or BUS1MIS

**About this subject:**  
Introduces the concept of an information system (IS). Covers the systems development life cycle (SDLC) as a framework for IS development, the role of users in IS projects, fact-finding techniques, data flow and entity-relationship modelling. Students build a small IS using a prototyping tool. Also covers file design, input/output/interface design, file conversion and implementation techniques.

**SILOs — upon successful completion, students should be able to:**
1. Use a generalised model of an information system (IS) to analyse, identify and effectively describe the components of a simple real-life information system.
2. Describe the Systems Development Life Cycle approach to developing IS.
3. Use established fact-finding techniques to elicit IS requirements for a simple business scenario.
4. Use Context and Level 0 Data Flow Diagrams and a System Dictionary to adequately describe the requirements for a simple IS.
5. Use Entity-Relationship Diagrams to design the database for a simple IS.
6. Critique the design of the user interface of a simple IS.
7. Justify the choice of an implementation approach and security requirements for a new IS.

**Weekly topics:** 12 weeks, Lecture + Tutorial + Lab + Online quiz per week (48 contact hours total)

**DCSPrep mapping:** SILO 2 (SDLC) and SILO 7 (security requirements for IS) have direct DCS relevance — understanding how systems are built and maintained, change management concepts. SILOs 3–6 are more analyst/developer focused. Useful for a "how schools choose and implement software systems" context module.

### CSE4002 — Artificial Intelligence Fundamentals
**Course:** SMITB (Core/Elective)  
**AQF Level:** 7 | **Credit Points:** 15 | **Delivery:** Day (Bendigo)  
**Prerequisites:** CSE4IP

**About this subject:**  
Introduces fundamental concepts and different application fields of AI. Main topics: searching, knowledge representation and reasoning, expert system design and development, responsible AI principles and applications. Hands-on lab practice designing and developing AI models for real-world problems.

**SILOs — upon successful completion, students should be able to:**
1. Devise appropriate representations for state space search and game playing to solve practical problems.
2. Represent knowledge using semantic networks, scripts, frames and logic-based mechanisms and write simple Prolog programs to enable automated reasoning.
3. Construct simple expert systems to solve a real-world problem.
4. Analyse and design basic machine learning algorithms to model a practical case.

**Weekly topics:**
1. Introduction to AI
2. Blind Search
3. Heuristic Search
4–5. Logic and Knowledge Representation
6. Automated Reasoning
7. Expert Systems
8–9. Machine Learning
10. AZURE: A Cloud-Based AI Platform
11. Game-Playing
12. Revision

**DCSPrep mapping:** Week 10 (Azure AI) has direct DCS relevance for school cloud context. SILO 4 (ML algorithms) connects to AI-powered IT tools students will encounter. Responsible AI principles (mentioned in description) are important for school data-handling context. Could seed a "AI in school IT — what it means for support staff" module.

### CSE5006 — Cloud-Based Web Application
**Course:** SMITB  
**AQF Level:** 7 | **Credit Points:** 15 | **Delivery:** Day (Bendigo)  
**Prerequisites:** CSE4IIP OR CSE4OOF  
**Assumed knowledge:** Basic JavaScript, CSS, HTML, database programming

**About this subject:**  
Focus on cloud computing concepts and tools needed to make websites scalable. Covers cloud computing concepts/technologies, cloud repository, virtualisation, frontend/backend development frameworks, API, web server storage, and CI/CD (Continuous Integration/Continuous Deployment). Covers cloud computing platform features.

**SILOs — upon successful completion, students should be able to:**
1. Design and develop web applications using JavaScript.
2. Design and build a stateless web server based on cloud technologies.
3. Design and customise backend web applications based on user requirements.
4. Use modern software engineering tools to build and deploy robust code for scalable websites.
5. Investigate storage technologies to determine optimal choice for a web site.

**Weekly topics:**
1. Git and Version Control Systems
2. Docker
3. JavaScript
4. Introduction to React _(mid-semester break)_
5. Object Relational Mapping
6. RESTful API
7–8. AWS Cloud Developing (Amazon S3)
9. AWS Cloud Container
10. Automating Deployment using CI/CD Pipelines in AWS
11. Revision

**DCSPrep mapping:** SILO 4 (software engineering tools, deployment) and Week 1 (Git/version control) connect to how school IT systems are maintained and updated. AWS/cloud topics underpin Microsoft 365 and Azure services used in schools. A "cloud platforms in schools" module could draw on this. CI/CD concepts explain automated software updates students see on managed devices.

### CSE5BDC — Big Data Management on the Cloud
**Course:** SMITB (co-taught with CSE3BDC undergraduate)  
**AQF Level:** Postgraduate | **Credit Points:** 15  
**Prerequisites:** CSE2DBF, (CSE1OOF or CSE1PE)  
**Assumed knowledge:** Programming in Java or Python, knowledge of databases

**About this subject:**  
Covers state-of-the-art technologies for analysing huge amounts of data and responding to millions of user requests. Uses Amazon Web Services (AWS) cloud services for big data. Covers Hadoop programming (MapReduce, Hive, Apache Spark), cloud architectures, parallel database systems, key-value stores, transaction support in the cloud, virtualisation, and multi-tenant database systems.

**SILOs — upon successful completion, students should be able to:**
1. Apply knowledge of Amazon Web Services to design a cloud-based solution for a given application scenario.
2. Implement fundamental cloud-based services using Amazon Web Service interfaces.
3. Analyse the internals of the MapReduce Framework.
4. Solve big data analytics problems by implementing solutions using the Hadoop ecosystem.
5. Analyse the performance benefits of contrasting NoSQL stores.

**Weekly topics:**
1. Introduction to cloud computing, cloud architectures, AWS basic services
2. Introduction to Big Data and MapReduce, AWS Elastic MapReduce
3. Hadoop internals, Advanced MapReduce
4. Pig and Hive (high-level Hadoop programming)
5. YARN, data streams, graph analytics
6. Introduction to Spark and Scala
7. Spark internals, Spark Programming
8. Spark SQL
9. Spark Streaming, Spark GraphX
10. Parallel Database Systems and NoSQL key-value stores
11. Different AWS services
12. Exam revision

**DCSPrep mapping:** SILOs 1–2 (AWS cloud solutions) have direct relevance to understanding school cloud infrastructure. The conceptual framing of "why databases are structured differently at scale" helps IT support staff understand why some systems behave the way they do. More of a background/context subject than a directly applicable one for L1 support.

### CSE5DL — Deep Learning
**Course:** SMITB  
**AQF Level:** Postgraduate | **Credit Points:** 15 | **Delivery:** Face-to-face (Bundoora)  
**Prerequisites:** CSE1OOF OR CSE5APG OR CSE4IP OR CSE1CPP OR CSE4OOF  
**Assumed knowledge:** Programming skills in some programming language

**About this subject:**  
Deep learning is the central machine learning method fuelling the AI revolution. Covers how to apply deep learning algorithms to real-world problems starting from an introductory level. Covers image recognition, sentiment classification, machine translation, question answering, speech synthesis. Introduces PyTorch and TensorFlow, reinforcement learning, generative adversarial networks (GANs), and few-shot learning.

**SILOs — upon successful completion, students should be able to:**
1. Analyse the advantages and disadvantages of using traditional machine learning algorithms versus deep learning algorithms for solving industry problems.
2. Analyse a given industry problem and recommend which deep learning algorithm best solves the problem.
3. Write/produce PyTorch code to implement deep learning algorithms to solve computer vision and natural language processing problems.
4. Propose deployment and maintenance strategies for deep learning production systems in the cloud based on analysis from machine learning requirements of a business.
5. Given a real-world problem where labels are scarce, investigate different deep learning approaches to tackle this problem.
6. Design the architecture of a generative adversarial network that can generate realistic data.

**Weekly topics:**
1. Introduction to Deep Learning
2. Foundations of Deep Learning
3. Deep Learning for Computer Vision
4. Practical Deep Learning
5. Deep Learning for Natural Language Processing
6. NLP in Practice
7. Deep Learning in Production
8. Unsupervised Deep Learning
9. Deep Learning for Time-Series Analysis and Deep Reinforcement Learning
10–12. (Advanced topics)

**DCSPrep mapping:** SILO 4 (deployment and maintenance in the cloud) has applicability to understanding managed AI tools in school environments. SILO 1 (ML vs deep learning trade-offs) is useful background for evaluating vendor AI claims in edtech procurement. More of a stretch/background subject for DCS PD context.

### CSE5ML — Machine Learning
**Course:** SMITB  
**AQF Level:** Postgraduate (Level 8) | **Credit Points:** 15 | **Delivery:** Online  
**Prerequisites:** CSE3CI or admission into SMIT/SMICT/SMITCN/SMCSC/SGIT/SGCS/SMDS/TM003

**About this subject:**  
Covers fundamentals of machine learning in theory and practice, focused on solving industrial data modelling problems using neural networks. Covers data regression and classification, testing various learning algorithms, comparing performance evaluations. Advanced ML techniques for data classification addressed. Hands-on lab work with industrial data modelling.

**SILOs — upon successful completion, students should be able to:**
1. Explain associated concepts and applications of machine learning techniques for data analytics.
2. Critically identify the major components and system design in developing neural networks for data regression and classification.
3. Analyse data to design, implement and evaluate machine learning techniques for real-world problem solving.
4. Implement a neural network with different learning algorithms for time-series forecasting with real-world data from industry.

**Weekly topics (7-week intensive, 15 hrs/week self-directed):**
1. Machine learning basics
2. Linear regression and support vector machines
3. Unsupervised learning
4. Deep learning basics and neural networks
5. Convolutional neural networks
6. Recurrent neural networks and LSTM
7. Study/Exam week

**DCSPrep mapping:** SILO 1 (ML concepts and applications) is increasingly relevant as schools adopt AI-based tools (Microsoft Copilot, AI-assisted marking, etc.). Understanding what ML is doing under the hood helps IT support staff answer teacher questions and identify when something is an AI limitation vs. a technical fault.

### CSE5NLP — Natural Language Processing
**Course:** SMITB  
**AQF Level:** Level 8 | **Credit Points:** 15 | **Delivery:** Online (Bundoora)  
**Prerequisites:** CSE5AIF  
**Assumed knowledge:** Python programming, machine learning, basic probability and statistics

**About this subject:**  
Broadly concerned with interactions between computers and natural (human) languages. Covers essential mathematical and linguistic concepts underlying NLP, then develops skills in tokenisation, morphological analysis, word sense disambiguation, part-of-speech tagging and sentence structure analysis. Applications: text categorisation, clustering, recommendation and information retrieval. Both lexical (dictionary-based) and machine learning approaches used.

**SILOs — upon successful completion, students should be able to:**
1. Apply NLP sub-tasks — including tokenisation, morphological analysis, word sense disambiguation, part-of-speech tagging and sentence structure analysis — to natural language texts.
2. Describe and evaluate the methods and algorithms used to process different types of textual data.
3. Devise NLP pipelines using existing NLP code libraries, text corpora and lexical resources such as WordNet.
4. Critically evaluate results of applying NLP methods to real-world tasks such as text categorisation, text clustering, text recommendation and information retrieval.

**Weekly topics:**
1. Introduction to NLP and NLP basic tasks
2. Text classification
3. Sequence labelling, vector semantics and embeddings
4. Neural networks and neural language models
5. Deep learning architectures (continuing)

**DCSPrep mapping:** NLP underpins tools like Microsoft Copilot, AI search, and chatbots increasingly present in school environments. SILO 4 (critically evaluate NLP results) is directly useful: understanding why an AI chatbot gave a wrong answer, or why search returned irrelevant results. Could seed a "how AI tools in Microsoft 365 work" module.

### CSE5CV — Computer Vision
**Course:** SMITB  
**AQF Level:** Level 9 — Masters Degree | **Credit Points:** 15 | **Delivery:** Daytime (Bundoora)  
**Prerequisites:** CSE4IP or CSE4002

**About this subject:**  
Computer vision is an interdisciplinary field dealing with how computers gain high-level understanding from digital images or videos. Covers camera imaging geometry, feature detection and matching, stereo, motion estimation and tracking, scene understanding and image captioning. Students practice statistical and machine learning models for computer vision tasks and implement algorithms for real-world applications in labs.

**SILOs — upon successful completion, students should be able to:**
1. Critique image processing techniques for basic computer vision tasks and address design issues.
2. Implement and analyse statistical and machine learning models for advanced computer vision tasks and address design issues.
3. Design, implement and evaluate a machine vision model to recognise visual concepts from images.
4. Analyse a given real-world problem and investigate different computer vision approaches to provide an optimal solution.

**Weekly topics:**
1. Introduction to computer vision, digital image fundamentals
2. Image features, histograms, contrast enhancement, denoising, filtering
3. Image feature extraction, feature-based classification, performance evaluation
4. Convolutional neural network classification models
5. Object detection and recognition
6. Face detection and recognition
7. Video classification and human pose estimation
8. Synthetic image generation
9. Microsoft Azure for computer vision
10. Virtual and augmented reality applications
11. Multimedia and computer graphics applications
12. Revision

**DCSPrep mapping:** Week 9 (Microsoft Azure for computer vision) is the most directly applicable — school surveillance, assistive tech, and Microsoft tools increasingly use computer vision. For DCS, understanding what "computer vision" means helps explain features like Windows Hello, camera-based accessibility tools, or document scanning in Microsoft 365.

### CSE3PE — Professional Environment
**Course:** RBC/SMITB (Professional practice subject)  
**AQF Level:** Undergraduate | **Credit Points:** 15 | **Delivery:** Day (Bundoora)  
**Prerequisites:** None

**About this subject:**  
Students become aware of the professional aspects of practising in the computing field, understand ethical and legal responsibilities of computing professionals, and consider the social implications of their work.

**SILOs — upon successful completion, students should be able to:**
1. Apply ethical theory to the resolution of ethical dilemmas in IT.
2. Develop a personal ethical framework for IT practice.
3. Investigate and intensively analyse significant contemporary social, legal and ethical issues in IT.
4. Apply the principles of critical thinking to social, legal and ethical issues in IT.
5. Appreciate the responsibilities of IT professionals to employers, clients and society.
6. Develop an appreciation of the importance of reflection in professional practice.

**Assessment:**
- 15% Individual presentation (Weeks 3–4)
- 10% Ethical Case Reflection submitted via LMS (Weeks 2–8)
- 15% Group Presentation (Weeks 10–11)
- 60% Final exam

**DCSPrep mapping:** This is the strongest alignment with the DCSPrep mission. SILOs 1–6 directly map to professional development values: ethical IT practice, legal responsibilities (privacy, data, copyright), social implications of school IT decisions, and reflection in practice. Could anchor an entire "Professional Responsibility in School IT" module cluster. SILO 6 (reflection in professional practice) is especially important for PD journal/evidence-pack features already in the app.

- **SLG dates vary:** CSE1IS and CSE3PE are 2018 SLGs; CSE1OOF is 2020; others are 2023–2025. SILOs in older SLGs may have been updated since. Treat 2018 SLGs as directional rather than definitive.
- **SMITB zip not separately included:** The SMITB postgraduate subjects (CSE4002, CSE5006, CSE5BDC, CSE5DL, CSE5ML, CSE5NLP, CSE5CV) came from the drive-download zip, not a dedicated SMITB folder. Confirm with Paul/subject coordinators which of these are actively taught at Bendigo vs. Bundoora only.
- **CSE4IP (Introduction to Programming, PG)** appears as a prerequisite for CSE4002 and CSE5006 but no SLG was included — this is likely the postgrad entry-level programming subject.
